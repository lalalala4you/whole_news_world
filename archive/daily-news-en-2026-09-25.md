## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | Xi feted at White House state dinner as Trump hails "great friendship"
President Donald Trump hosted Xi Jinping for a state dinner at the White House on Thursday, with Tesla's Elon Musk and OpenAI's Sam Altman among the business guests, capping a summit heavy on pageantry but light on breakthroughs. Xi announced a gift of Chinese pandas and a new student exchange programme, while Trump said the talks would cover "security, technology and superintelligence." On the sidelines, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the year-long trade truce between the two economies had been extended by another two months, with analysts expecting a further extension before the visit ends. Xi again invoked the "Thucydides trap," warning that shifts in great-power balance can lead to conflict, and the two sides remain far apart on the Iran war, Ukraine and AI regulation.

**Why it matters:** A rare face-to-face between the world's two largest economies shapes tariffs, AI governance and the balance of power across the Indo-Pacific.

### #2 | Netanyahu defiant at UN as delegates walk out; Abbas warns two-state solution at risk
Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu defended Israel's military campaigns in Iran, Gaza and the wider Middle East in a defiant UN General Assembly speech, after dozens of delegates walked out in protest. He dismissed those who left as "moral cowards," rejected accusations of genocide in Gaza, called Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan a "tyrant," and thanked Trump for US-Israeli strikes on Tehran in February. Hours earlier, Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas — speaking by video link after being denied a US visa — said Israeli settlement expansion in the occupied West Bank threatened "the very life and existence of the Palestinian people" and jeopardised the two-state solution. Nearly a year after a ceasefire took hold, reconstruction in Gaza has yet to begin, and Israel is heading toward elections next month.

**Why it matters:** Israel's growing diplomatic isolation and the stalled Gaza recovery are testing the durability of the US-brokered ceasefire.

🏷️ Middle East / Diplomacy

### #3 | Poland calls Starlink station fire sabotage as Denmark warns of Russian threat
Polish Deputy Prime Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski said a fire at a state-owned Starlink satellite station in Wola Krobowska was a deliberate "act of sabotage" designed to disrupt internet access for institutions including the Ukrainian army, and that "many indications" point to a "new Russian doctrine of attack." The Kremlin has not commented. Separately, Denmark's intelligence service issued a new assessment saying it expects Russia to escalate hybrid warfare in the coming months and warned of a "low but growing risk" of a limited military attack on a NATO country. Moldova's president said four Russian drones crossed its airspace with at least one exploding, Romania reported a drone crashing on its territory, and a Russian strike on a farm near Kharkiv killed six people.

**Why it matters:** A widening pattern of sabotage and airspace violations keeps Europe on edge about how far Moscow will push short of open war with NATO.

🏷️ Security / Europe

### #4 | Rebel offensive in Ethiopia stokes fears of a return to civil war
Fighting has escalated between Ethiopian government forces and Tigrayan rebels after the rebels joined a new coalition aimed at toppling Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government, raising fears of a return to full-scale civil war. The alliance brings together movements with different histories, claims and enemies, complicating an already fragile security picture in the Horn of Africa. The offensive marks one of the most serious challenges to Abiy since the 2022 Tigray peace deal ended two years of devastating conflict. Aid agencies warn that renewed fighting could displace large numbers of civilians in a region already hosting millions of refugees from neighbouring Sudan.

**Why it matters:** A renewed war in Ethiopia would destabilise the Horn of Africa and deepen one of the world's largest humanitarian crises.

🏷️ Conflict / Africa

### #5 | Iran warns war could spread to the Indian Ocean if the US strikes again
An adviser to Iran's supreme leader, Yahya Rahim Safavi, warned that Tehran may expand the Middle East conflict to the Indian Ocean and "perhaps beyond" if the United States attacks again, in a video released by the Fars news agency. It is the first time an adviser to the supreme leader has explicitly named the Indian Ocean, home to the strategic US-British base at Diego Garcia, some 4,000km from Iran. Iran has locked down the Strait of Hormuz in retaliation for US-Israeli strikes that began the war in late February, while Yemen's Houthis have tightened their grip on the Red Sea and the Bab al-Mandab Strait. US and Iranian officials held talks in New York this week on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, with Tehran demanding a lifting of sanctions and the release of frozen assets.

**Why it matters:** Threats to Hormuz, the Red Sea and now the Indian Ocean put global oil and gas shipping — and world energy prices — squarely in the crosshairs.
