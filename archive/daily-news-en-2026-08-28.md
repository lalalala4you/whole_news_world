## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | Glacial collapse floods kill at least 489 along Nepal–Tibet border, with 1,300+ missing
A collapsed glacier triggered catastrophic flash floods and landslides along the Nepal–Tibet border on Wednesday, killing at least 489 people and leaving more than 1,300 missing. Officials said people from more than 30 countries are among the missing, including dozens of Americans and Canadians and 33 British nationals, while Beijing warned that a newly formed landslide lake near the border could burst its banks and cause a second flood. Rescue teams are still working to reach survivors, including more than 100 people feared trapped in a hydropower tunnel in Rasuwa.

**Why it matters:** A cross-border natural disaster with casualties spanning more than 30 nationalities is testing rescue and humanitarian coordination between China and Nepal — and could worsen if a second flood is unleashed.

### #2 | Trump's war with Iran hits six months as US Patriot stocks in Europe run "beyond critical"
President Donald Trump's war with Iran reached the six-month mark on Friday, with the conflict's ripple effects now straining NATO's own defenses. A US defense official told AP that American Patriot missile interceptor stocks in Europe are "beyond critical," largely because interceptors were shifted to the Middle East to shoot down Iranian drones and missiles. Separately, CIA Director John Ratcliffe traveled to Moscow this week to warn Russia against attacking NATO territory, while Washington threatened to sanction countries that refuse to break trade ties with Tehran.

**Why it matters:** The six-month conflict is no longer contained to the Middle East — it is depleting the missile defenses that protect NATO members and leaving Europe more exposed to a potential Russian attack.

🏷️ Middle East / Defense

### #3 | Norway's King Harald V dies aged 89 after 35 years on the throne
Norway's King Harald V died peacefully at 06:35 local time on Friday at the national hospital in Oslo, aged 89, after being treated for a rare blood condition. The first Norwegian-born monarch since the 14th century, Harald reigned for 35 years and was widely loved as a down-to-earth "People's King" who modernised the monarchy and broke with tradition by marrying a commoner. He is succeeded by his son, King Haakon, 53, who will hold an extraordinary council of state with the government.

**Why it matters:** Norway loses its longest-serving unifying figure, whose reign spanned national tragedies and a modernising, increasingly egalitarian monarchy now entering a period of transition.

🏷️ Europe

### #4 | Trump signs order renaming Lake Ontario as "Lake America" amid deepening Canada feud
US President Donald Trump signed an executive order seeking to rename Lake Ontario — the smallest of the Great Lakes, shared with Canada — as "Lake America." The move comes after US–Canada trade talks collapsed and Washington imposed new 50% tariffs on $20 billion (C$28 billion) of Canadian goods, with Canada pledging "dollar for dollar" counter-tariffs next month. Prime Minister Mark Carney rejected the change, insisting the lake "is called Lake Ontario — today and forever," while Ontario Premier Doug Ford and other officials dismissed the order.

**Why it matters:** The symbolic renaming, following the Gulf of Mexico controversy, marks a further escalation of the unprecedented rift between two of the world's most integrated economies.

🏷️ North America / Trade

### #5 | Rohingya refugees in Malaysia face fierce new wave of hostility
A resurgent wave of hostility is sweeping Malaysia against its Rohingya community, one of the world's largest stateless populations, with more than 126,000 resettled in the country according to the UN. Community-run schools have been raided, families evicted from villages they had lived in for years, and Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim recently said Myanmar had agreed to take back 5,000 refugees. The UN warned this week that the plight of minorities in Myanmar has "plummeted to a new low," while activists say the Rohingya are being scapegoated for Malaysia's rising cost of living.

**Why it matters:** The deteriorating treatment of a stateless minority that fled genocide underscores a widening regional refugee crisis, as Myanmar remains locked in civil war and safe havens grow fewer.
