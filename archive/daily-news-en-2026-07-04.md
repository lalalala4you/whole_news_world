### #1 | Iran: Khamenei's Six-Day Funeral Begins as Millions Gather in Tehran

Thousands of black-clad mourners gathered outside Tehran's Grand Mosalla on Saturday for the first day of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei's funeral, more than four months after he was killed in joint US and Israeli strikes. Authorities expect 15–20 million people to attend the six-day ceremonies across Iran and Iraq, with Khamenei's body lying in state for three days before travelling to the holy city of Qom, then Najaf and Karbala in Iraq, and finally burial in Mashhad next Thursday. Mourners chanted anti-US slogans and vowed revenge, with one attendee telling Reuters, "Everyone here has come to avenge the blood of their supreme leader." Trump said Iran's government was "dying to settle" a peace deal and had been given "a week off for a funeral because we're nice." Khamenei's successor, his son Mojtaba, has not been seen in public since assuming the role, and whether he appears at the funeral remains a key question.

**Why it matters:** The funeral marks a pivotal moment in Iran's post-Khamenei transition amid an unresolved conflict with the US and Israel, with implications for Middle East stability and global oil markets.

### #2 | US Turns 250: Trump Marks Independence Day with Partisan Speech and Record Fireworks

The United States celebrated its 250th anniversary on Saturday with 850,000 fireworks across Washington DC, hourly military flyovers featuring the new Air Force One, and a "really long speech" from President Trump — all under scorching 38°C (100°F) heat. On the eve of Independence Day, Trump used a rally at Mount Rushmore to claim American identity was under threat from "radicals," "extremists," and what he called "the communist menace," in what critics described as a deeply partisan politicization of the national milestone. The White House-backed Freedom250 organization has been accused of supplanting Congress's bipartisan America250 commission. Concerns over crowd size, air pollution from the record firework display, and potential thunderstorms also cloud the celebrations. In New York, the Times Square Ball will drop eight times, while Philadelphia — where the Declaration of Independence was signed — hosts a concert featuring Christina Aguilera and Meek Mill.

**Why it matters:** The semiquincentennial reveals deep political fractures in America as Trump uses the historic occasion to frame the November midterms around a culture-war narrative.

### #3 | Germany Summons Chinese Ambassador Over Reports of Russian Soldiers Training in China

Germany summoned China's ambassador in Berlin on Friday over what it called "deeply disturbing" reports that the Chinese People's Liberation Army has been secretly training Russian soldiers on its territory, some of whom were later deployed in Ukraine. German daily Die Welt first reported the classified intelligence findings in May, and the German foreign ministry said "anything that enables Russia to continue its war of aggression against Ukraine also represents a threat to our security." The diplomatic escalation comes as Russia also claimed full control of Kostiantynivka in eastern Ukraine — a city of 78,000 — and launched a glide bomb strike on Sumy that killed at least four civilians. Meanwhile, Lithuania's president called for integration into Western nuclear deterrence and initiated a constitutional amendment to allow nuclear weapons deployment on its soil.

**Why it matters:** Confirmation of China directly training Russian combat troops would mark a major escalation in Beijing's alignment with Moscow, potentially reshaping NATO's strategic posture toward China.

### #4 | Record-Breaking Heatwaves Grip US and Europe as Climate Crisis Deepens

More than 165 million Americans are under dangerous heat alerts on Independence Day, with temperatures reaching 38°C (100°F) and forcing the cancellation of Fourth of July events from Washington DC to Philadelphia. The heat follows Europe's "extraordinary" June, during which temperature records were not just broken but shattered — the UK hit 37.7°C in Norfolk, smashing its previous June high of 35.6°C by more than 2°C, while more than a dozen European countries broke their June records by up to 3°C. Scientists called the events a direct signature of human-caused climate change. "Our heatwaves will get hotter and hotter and hotter until we get to global net zero," said Professor Ed Hawkins of the University of Reading. Europe's seas are also at record-warm levels, reducing the cooling effect of coastal breezes and intensifying land heat.

**Why it matters:** The simultaneous extreme heat events across two continents underscore the accelerating pace of climate change and the mounting human and economic costs of delayed action.

The England team were booed on arrival at their hotel. Meanwhile, the German FA is seeking talks with Jürgen Klopp after Julian Nagelsmann resigned following Germany's early exit.

**Why it matters:** The World Cup in North America is the planet's most-watched event, and the knockout-stage drama is uniting — and dividing — fans across every continent.
