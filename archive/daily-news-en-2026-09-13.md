### #1 | Commercial vessel struck in Strait of Hormuz, killing one as US-Iran tensions persist
Iran reported that a commercial vessel was struck in the Strait of Hormuz, killing one person and wounding four, in the latest incident along a waterway at the center of the US-Iran conflict. **Why it matters:** A fresh attack on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz deepens the risk to the world's most critical oil transit corridor and dims hopes for a diplomatic off-ramp.

### #2 | Canada makes audacious bid to become an EU 'associate member' amid US trade war
Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney is pursuing a dramatic bid to make Canada an "associate member" of the European Union, a move framed as a pivot away from the United States as the two countries' trade war escalates. The proposal drew skepticism from some quarters even as it signals a historic reorientation of Canada's alliances. It comes as Canadian consumers increasingly shun US goods.

**Why it matters:** Canada's outreach to Europe reflects how sharply the North American relationship has deteriorated, with potentially lasting consequences for global trade alliances.

### #3 | Search underway for about 130 people after Indonesian passenger ship overturns
An Indonesian passenger ship carrying more than 240 people overturned in the Java Sea, leaving at least six dead and roughly 130 unaccounted for as rescue teams raced to find survivors. The vessel lost contact before the accident, and authorities are searching the area for the missing. The disaster is the latest in a series of maritime accidents in the region.

**Why it matters:** A large-scale maritime tragedy with well over a hundred missing underscores persistent ferry-safety challenges in Indonesia.

**Why it matters:** A prominent gubernatorial candidate's brush with disaster is a striking personal story amid a high-profile campaign.

A growing number of Canadians are boycotting US products and avoiding travel south as President Trump's rhetoric and tariffs strain the historic alliance, a trend captured in the phrase "Bye, America." Grocers are adapting and seeking new supply sources as consumer sentiment shifts. The widening trade war is reshaping everyday habits on both sides of the border.

**Why it matters:** The cultural and economic backlash in Canada is a concrete sign of how deeply the US-Canada relationship has frayed.
