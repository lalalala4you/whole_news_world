## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | UN Experts Say US Strikes on Iran May Amount to War Crimes

UN human rights experts say there are grounds to believe the United States committed war crimes in its strikes on Iran, pointing to February's deadly attack on a primary school in Minab and a sports complex in Lamerd. Washington has not acknowledged carrying out either strike, and no independent verification of the incidents has been made public by US officials. The finding lands as the conflict passes its half-year mark, with Washington set to allow an Iranian delegation to attend UN meetings in New York.

**Why it matters:** An expert finding of possible US war crimes sharply raises the legal and diplomatic pressure on Washington and narrows the room for de-escalation with Tehran.

### #2 | Russians Vote in Duma Elections as Poland Warns of New Hybrid Attacks

Russians went to the polls in parliamentary elections in which President Vladimir Putin's United Russia party faces no meaningful opposition, after the liberal anti-war party was barred from the ballot. Poland's prime minister, Donald Tusk, travelled to Kyiv after Warsaw warned that Moscow is planning "hybrid strikes" against countries supporting Ukraine. On the battlefield, Ukrainian drones killed a Russian commander who had just been decorated following a disputed victory claim.

**Why it matters:** The vote cements Putin's domestic control while an escalating Russian hybrid campaign across Europe tests Nato's unity and its red lines.

🏷️ Russia & Europe

### #3 | Trump Threatens EU Trade Over Canada's 'Associate Membership' Offer

President Donald Trump called the European Union's offer of "associate membership" to Canada "laughable" and threatened to stop trading with the bloc, after Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney embraced the proposal in Strasbourg. European leaders responded with warm words but offered no guarantees that the idea will ever get off the ground, let alone become a template for other countries. The row piles fresh strain onto already tense transatlantic trade relations.

**Why it matters:** A US–EU trade rupture would disrupt global supply chains and test whether Europe can convert diplomatic goodwill into durable alternative alliances.

🏷️ Trade & Diplomacy

### #4 | Japan Raises Interest Rate to a New 31-Year High

Japan's central bank raised interest rates to their highest level in 31 years in a bid to curb rising prices, as high energy costs push inflation up around the world. The move extends Tokyo's gradual exit from decades of ultra-loose monetary policy. It follows similar tightening by central banks in other economies also grappling with energy-driven inflation.

**Why it matters:** Higher Japanese rates ripple through global bond markets and squeeze the yen-funded carry trades that underpin much of the world's liquidity.

🏷️ Economy

### #5 | Sweden's PM Resigns After Centre-Left Bloc Wins Slim Majority

Sweden's prime minister has resigned after the centre-left bloc won a slim majority in the country's general election. The result brings an end to the outgoing conservative-led government's term in office. The winning bloc now faces the task of forming a stable administration with a narrow mandate.

**Why it matters:** Sweden's political shift redraws part of Europe's map at a moment of defence buildup and heightened Nato–Russia tension.
