### #1 | Yemen war escalates as Houthis advance and UK moves to back Saudi Arabia
Fighting on Yemen's west coast has driven thousands of civilians to flee by boat across the Red Sea to Djibouti, as Houthi forces move to seize strategic terrain after President Trump called off US airstrikes at the last minute. Thick smoke was seen near Riyadh's airport after the Houthis claimed an attack on the Saudi capital. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Britain will provide Saudi Arabia with "defensive" military support, including air-to-air refuelling, following a request from Riyadh. Iran meanwhile claims Washington is preparing to renew its bombing campaign, while Macron described his talks with Trump on the Red Sea as "constructive."

**Why it matters:** The escalation pulls the US, UK, Saudi Arabia and Iran deeper into a conflict that threatens Red Sea shipping lanes and global oil flows.

### #2 | US networks sue Trump over White House press ban — and suspend pool coverage
CNN, MS NOW and Politico have filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration's ban on their access to the White House, and four other networks that make up the White House TV pool said they would stop covering the president in solidarity. Trump said he "probably" will lose the lawsuit, blaming the judge he said was appointed by "TRUMP," while continuing his "fake news" attacks. The standoff intensified as Trump travelled to New York for the UN General Assembly. The dispute has become the sharpest test in years of press access to the presidency.

**Why it matters:** A coordinated media revolt against White House access restrictions sets a precedent for press freedom and how the US presidency is covered globally.

### #3 | Typhoon Dujuan batters Japan, triggering mass evacuation orders
Typhoon Dujuan swept along Japan's eastern coast, bringing heavy rain and major disruption, after the country's meteorological agency warned of an "imminent threat to life" — a warning later downgraded. Millions of residents were urged to evacuate as the storm barrelled toward the main islands. Forecasters expect the system to restrengthen into a typhoon again and hit the Izu Islands next. Airlines and rail operators reported cancellations and delays across the affected regions.

**Why it matters:** One of the season's most powerful storms is testing Japan's disaster preparedness and disrupting travel and supply chains across East Asia.

### #4 | Germany's Merz on the brink after far-right AfD surge in state elections
Chancellor Friedrich Merz's CDU suffered heavy losses in two state elections and was ejected from the Mecklenburg parliament, delivering another blow from the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD). Merz vowed to stay on for Germany's "democratic future," and the centre-left SPD signalled it wants to remain in the governing coalition. The results deepen uncertainty in Europe's largest economy and add to pressure on Berlin's leadership. European spy chiefs separately warned that Moscow is planning more decisive action against NATO.

**Why it matters:** A weakened German government and rising far-right influence complicate Europe's response to Russia, the war in Ukraine and transatlantic tensions.

🏷️ Europe / Politics

### #5 | Record-breaking El Niño shatters heat records as scientists warn of breached "planetary boundaries"
Scientists described a "jaw-dropping" El Niño that has burst through temperature records months before its expected peak, with world leaders addressing the climate crisis ahead of the UN General Assembly. A separate study warned that "planetary boundaries" necessary for the flourishing of life have been massively breached. The record heat is compounding extreme weather, from wildfires in Colombia's tourist heartland to devastating floods in Nepal and Tibet linked to a weakened glacier. Researchers say the full impact has yet to arrive.

**Why it matters:** Accelerating climate extremes are reshaping economies, food and energy security, and disaster planning across every region.
