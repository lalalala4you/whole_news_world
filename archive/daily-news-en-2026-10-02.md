## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | Flydubai co-pilot stabbing: injured 'hero' pilot saves 174 lives mid-air
A Dubai–Tel Aviv flydubai flight plunged nearly 14,000ft (4,300m) in under 30 seconds after one pilot stabbed his colleague and apparently tried to crash the plane. Despite grievous wounds, Capt Smit Machchhar, an Indian national from Mumbai, forced open the cockpit door so passengers and crew — including a plumber, a dentist and off-duty relief pilots — could overpower the attacker and stabilise the jet. The aircraft landed safely in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, with all 174 passengers surviving; Machchhar is stable after surgery. Israeli PM Benjamin Netanyahu said the attacker had "Islamist radical indoctrination," while US President Donald Trump vowed to hit Iran "very hard" if it were linked to the plot.

**Why it matters:** An aviation-security drama has become a flashpoint in the already volatile Israel–Iran–US confrontation.

### #2 | Putin threatens 'all weapons' to defend Kaliningrad
Speaking at the Valdai foreign policy forum, Vladimir Putin said Russia would use every weapon at its disposal — including nuclear arms — if its Baltic exclave of Kaliningrad came under direct attack or blockade, while insisting Moscow is "not threatening anyone" and is merely responding to Western talk of war in 2029–30. NATO chief Mark Rutte said alliance exercises pose no threat and urged Moscow to stop its nuclear rhetoric. Russia struck a key bridge in central Kyiv with drones on Thursday, and Ukraine pressed on with strikes on Russian oil refineries.

**Why it matters:** New nuclear signalling over a NATO-flanked exclave sharply raises the risk of miscalculation in Europe.

🏷️ Europe / Security

### #3 | France's school protests escalate into 'urban violence'
Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu convened a crisis cabinet meeting after student demonstrations over crowded classes, teacher shortages and crumbling facilities spiralled nationwide. Police reported about 900 protests and blockades, with more than 160 schools shut and roughly 30 university campuses blocked; nearly 3,000 lycée students have been detained since the unrest began, including 1,949 on Thursday alone. More than 300 police and dozens of firefighters, teachers and pupils have been injured, and arson was reported at schools in Nantes, Marseille, Lille and Toulouse. The government blames the radical-left La France Insoumise, whose leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon denies endorsing violence.

**Why it matters:** A youth-led revolt over public education is testing an already fragile French government and its authority.

### #4 | Trump meddling row engulfs Brazil ahead of elections
Brazil's attorney general, Jorge Messias, said foreign meddling "cannot be tolerated," calling a Guardian investigation into Trump administration plans an "important alert for Brazilian sovereignty" as the country heads toward the polls. The reporting says Washington is diverting human rights funds to push a far-right agenda abroad, including support for white Afrikaners and anti-communist causes. A new Datafolha poll shows leftist incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva leading his right-wing rival by three points in a runoff scenario.

**Why it matters:** Washington's open alignment with Brazil's right could reshape Latin America's largest democracy and US–Brazil relations.

### #5 | MI5 warns UK universities over Chinese tech theft
Britain's domestic intelligence service urged universities to sever ties with the China General Technology Research Institute, an entity linked to China's Ministry of State Security, warning that UK-funded research is being passed to Beijing's spies. The alert is expected to send a chill through cash-strapped British universities that rely heavily on Chinese students and research money.

**Why it matters:** It hardens the West's tech-security rift with China, pitting academic openness against national-security concerns.
