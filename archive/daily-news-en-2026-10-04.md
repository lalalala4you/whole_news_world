## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | Russia hits second major bridge in Kyiv as strikes on critical infrastructure escalate
A Russian drone strike on Saturday damaged Kyiv's Pivnichnyi (Northern) Bridge, injuring two people and severing road, trolleybus and overhead wires across the Dnipro River, Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko said. It is the second major bridge hit in days, after a Friday attack on the Pivdennyi Bridge, and Moscow said its drones targeted crossings used to move Ukrainian troops and military cargo. Traffic is jammed across the capital for a second day, with around three million residents increasingly dependent on the metro. Ukraine's defence council held a crisis meeting and Klitschko warned the city faces a "very dramatic situation."

**Why it matters:** A sustained campaign against Kyiv's river crossings threatens to effectively cut the capital in two and marks a new phase in Russia's war on Ukraine's infrastructure.

### #2 | Flydubai co-pilot used crash axe to attack captain, UAE says, in declared terror incident
The United Arab Emirates' attorney general said the co-pilot who tried to seize a Flydubai jet bound for Tel Aviv attacked the captain with a crash axe, calling it an attempted "terrorist attack." Flight 1073, carrying more than 170 people from Dubai to Tel Aviv, plunged about 17,000 feet before passengers overpowered the attacker and two reserve pilots landed safely in Saudi Arabia. Captain Smit Machchhar was praised by India for his "immense courage." The suspect, named in multiple outlets as Hamam al-Hammami, was reportedly removed from training at Oman Air over radical views, and Israel's PM said he appeared to have undergone Islamist indoctrination.

**Why it matters:** The attack on a sensitive Gulf–Israel route raises fresh questions about aviation security vetting and screening of flight crews.

### #3 | North Korea fires ballistic missile after Seoul demands apology over wounded soldiers
North Korea launched a ballistic missile towards the sea, a day after Kim Yo-jong, the powerful sister of leader Kim Jong-un, denounced South Korea for claiming the North's mines wounded South Korean soldiers in the Demilitarized Zone. Seoul had demanded an apology over the incident, which sharply raised tensions along the heavily fortified border. The launch is Pyongyang's latest show of force amid a hardening cycle of provocation and rhetoric with the South.

**Why it matters:** Another missile test deepens inter-Korean tensions and complicates regional diplomacy already strained by the North's weapons programme.

🏷️ Asia / Security

### #4 | Tens of thousands protest Spain's housing crisis as snap election looms
More than 50 protests took place across Spain on Saturday as tens of thousands of people marched over soaring housing costs, after Congress rejected emergency legislation aimed at tackling the crisis. Demonstrators in Madrid and other cities accuse the government of failing to make housing affordable. The unrest has fuelled speculation that Spain could be heading for a snap general election.

**Why it matters:** Housing has become a potent political flashpoint across Europe, and sustained protests in one of the EU's largest economies could reshape its political landscape.

🏷️ Europe / Politics

### #5 | Brazil's neck-and-neck election tests Latin America's pro-Trump bloc
Brazil is heading into a closely contested presidential vote, with President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva arguing he is the only figure who can stop the rise of "fascism" under a Bolsonaro-family candidate. For the third election in a row, a member of the Bolsonaro family is the leading right-wing contender, in a race that has drawn Washington's attention. The contest is being watched as a test of the growing pro-Trump political bloc across Latin America.

**Why it matters:** A shift in Brazil, the region's largest economy and democracy, would reverberate across Latin America and shape its relations with the United States.
