## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | Israel admits soldiers fired at car that killed Gaza girl Hind Rajab; Australia summons envoy over aid worker ruling
Israel confirmed that its soldiers fired at the car in which five-year-old Hind Rajab was killed in Gaza in January 2024, and opened a criminal investigation into her death and that of six relatives who were with her. In a separate row, Australia's Foreign Minister Penny Wong said she would summon the Israeli ambassador after Israel declined to prosecute anyone over the killing of Australian aid worker Zomi Frankcom. The developments came as BBC Verify documented multiple deadly Israeli strikes across Gaza within a 24-hour period.

**Why it matters:** Ten months after the ceasefire, unresolved questions over civilian deaths in Gaza are straining Israel's ties with key allies like Australia.

🏷️ Middle East

### #2 | Trump says he will meet Kim Jong-un this year — as Kim's sister casts doubt on the claim
President Donald Trump said he plans to meet North Korean leader Kim Jong-un later this year, citing a "very good" relationship, and announced plans to curtail joint US-South Korea military exercises. South Korea subsequently shortened its war games with the US, citing Washington's request. But Kim Yo-jong, Kim Jong-un's sister, dismissed reports of secret US-North Korea talks, insisting the US remains Pyongyang's enemy — a contradiction that has left Seoul uneasy about the future of its alliance with Washington.

**Why it matters:** A potential Trump-Kim summit could upend security arrangements in Northeast Asia, where some 28,000 US troops are stationed in South Korea.

🏷️ Geopolitics

### #3 | US gross national debt tops $40 trillion for the first time
The United States' gross national debt surpassed $40 trillion for the first time, according to official figures — roughly doubling in about a decade amid years of deficit spending. The milestone lands as the Federal Reserve keeps rates elevated to fight inflation; minutes from the Fed's July meeting showed policymakers' inflation concerns had increased. Economists warn that rising debt-service costs will increasingly crowd out other federal spending.

**Why it matters:** The record debt load intensifies the debate over US fiscal sustainability ahead of the midterm elections and as borrowing costs stay high.

🏷️ Economy

### #4 | Zelenskyy sacks senior aide amid Ukraine corruption crisis
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy dismissed Deputy Chief of Staff Iryna Mudra, a day after his ousted former defence minister publicly called for a wartime presidential election. The shake-up comes amid a reported corruption investigation inside the presidential office that has turned former loyalists into vocal political opponents. Analysts say the scandals are testing the stability of Ukraine's leadership at a critical point in the war with Russia.

**Why it matters:** Wartime corruption allegations risk eroding public trust and complicating Western support for Ukraine.

🏷️ Europe / Ukraine

### #5 | Unitree surges 600% on debut as world's biggest humanoid robot maker lists in Shanghai
Shares in Chinese robotics firm Unitree, the world's largest maker of humanoid robots, surged as much as 600% on their first day of trading on Shanghai's STAR Market. The blockbuster debut caps months of surging investor enthusiasm for robotics and AI in China, with the company now valued in the tens of billions of dollars. The rally highlights both the promise of humanoid robots and concerns about frothy valuations in the sector.

**Why it matters:** The listing underscores China's push to lead the global humanoid robotics race — and the speculative fervor building around it.
