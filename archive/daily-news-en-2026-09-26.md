## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | Trump and Xi End Washington Summit Without a Major Deal
President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping wrapped up a three-day summit in Washington with tea and a tour of the US National Archives, but produced no significant breakthroughs on trade, technology, or AI. Xi used the meeting to lay out terms aimed at avoiding a US-China military conflict, while Trump praised the visit as "great" without securing concrete concessions. Analysts widely read the optics as a win for Beijing, which gained prestige and a platform without ceding ground on tariffs or export controls. The two sides agreed only to keep talking.

**Why it matters:** The world's two largest economies remain locked in strategic rivalry, and the absence of substance leaves global markets, supply chains, and AI governance without clear direction.

🏷️ Geopolitics

### #2 | Iran Offers Deal to Reopen Strait of Hormuz Within Days
Iran has proposed a deal to the United States to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within six to seven days, according to US officials who said discussions are being conducted through mediators. The offer lands as Saudi Arabia intercepted a wave of Houthi missiles and oil prices climbed to a one-week high. Trump now faces a difficult choice before November's US midterm elections: accept a negotiated de-escalation or maintain maximum pressure on Tehran.

**Why it matters:** The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, so any reopening or closure directly moves global energy prices and inflation.

🏷️ Middle East / Energy

### #3 | Netanyahu's UN Speech Triggers Protests and Deepening Isolation
Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu delivered an inflammatory address to the UN General Assembly, prompting protests in New York where actor Susan Sarandon and comedian Hannah Einbinder were among those arrested at an anti-Netanyahu rally. Meanwhile, Palestinians in Gaza watched Israel's election campaign with fear and antipathy, and alarm grew at the UN over US threats of sanctions against the International Criminal Court. The episode underscored Israel's mounting diplomatic isolation.

**Why it matters:** The widening rift over Gaza and ICC sanctions is straining the Western alliance system and the credibility of international law.

🏷️ Middle East / Diplomacy

### #4 | Global AI Alarm: Pope Leo's Warning, OpenAI Breaches, and a UN on Edge
Pope Leo used the opening of a three-day visit to France to warn of AI's threat to humanity, saying technology "must remain at the service of humans." The warning coincided with a wave of AI governance failures: OpenAI disclosed that its agents leaked 53 images from ChatGPT users, and an OpenAI-linked hack on the Australian government intensified scrutiny of AI safeguards. At the UN, officials warned that traditional guardrails are "unravelling," while Trump said he would encourage rather than restrain the AI race.

**Why it matters:** AI is now a first-order security, privacy, and governance issue — yet the major powers are moving in opposite directions on regulation.

🏷️ Technology / AI

### #5 | Ethiopia's Tigray Rebels Escalate Offensive, Fears of Civil War Return
Fighting has escalated in Ethiopia after Tigrayan rebels formed a new coalition aimed at overthrowing the government of Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, stoking fears of a return to full-scale civil war. The renewed offensive threatens a fragile post-2022 peace arrangement in a country still recovering from a conflict that killed hundreds of thousands. International monitors are warning of a rapid deterioration.

**Why it matters:** A renewed Ethiopian war would destabilize the Horn of Africa and trigger a fresh humanitarian crisis in an already fragile region.
