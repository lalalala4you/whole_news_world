## 🌍 Top 5 Global Stories

### #1 | US and Iran Trade Strikes as Strait of Hormuz Crisis Deepens
Tensions in the Middle East escalated dramatically on Tuesday as the United States and Iran exchanged a fresh wave of military strikes, with Iranian state media reporting explosions near multiple port cities. In a significant development, President Trump announced on Truth Social that he would scrap his controversial plan to impose a 20% fee on all cargo transiting the Strait of Hormuz, replacing it with "Trade and Investment Deals" with Gulf states. However, he simultaneously declared a "full blockade" on ships entering or leaving Iranian ports — a move Tehran called "an act of war." The confrontation has already disrupted commercial shipping through the critical waterway, with maritime security experts reporting that vessel owners are making "hour by hour decisions" on whether to transit. Oil prices surged further, while government borrowing costs rose in the UK and across Europe amid fears that prolonged instability will fuel inflation.

**Why it matters:** The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly one-fifth of the world's oil supply; a sustained disruption could trigger a global energy crisis and send shockwaves through the world economy.

### #2 | Yemen's Houthis Fire Missiles at Saudi Arabia After Sanaa Airport Strikes
Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement launched ballistic missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia's Abha International Airport on Monday night, in what marks the most serious escalation in the largely dormant conflict since an informal truce took effect four years ago. The Houthi retaliation followed air strikes on Sanaa's international airport, which Yemen's internationally recognised government said it carried out to prevent an Iranian plane carrying a Houthi delegation from landing. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences "dealt with" the incoming missiles and no casualties were reported, but the exchange has shattered the fragile calm that had held since 2022. At an emergency UN Security Council meeting, Assistant Secretary General Khaled Khiari warned that "Yemen and the wider region cannot afford another cycle of escalation."

**Why it matters:** The renewed Saudi-Houthi hostilities risk plunging Yemen — already facing one of the world's worst humanitarian crises — back into full-scale war, while further destabilising an already volatile Middle East.

🏷️ Middle East / Conflict / Yemen

### #3 | UK Covid Inquiry Finds £10bn Wasted on PPE That Left NHS Staff Poorly Protected
The UK's Covid-19 inquiry has found that almost £10 billion of taxpayers' money was wasted on personal protective equipment (PPE) during the pandemic, with two-thirds of the £14.9 billion spent by the UK and devolved governments going to supplies that were either unused or out of date. Inquiry chair Baroness Hallett described the "vast" waste as a consequence of the country entering the pandemic with its emergency stockpile in a "perilous state." The controversial "VIP lane" — which prioritised PPE offers from those with political connections — was condemned as a "misguided" policy that "embedded unfairness in emergency procurement." However, the inquiry found "no evidence of cronyism or corruption" by ministers when awarding final contracts. In total, the government spent over £42 billion on PPE, testing kits, and equipment between January 2020 and June 2022.

**Why it matters:** The inquiry's findings expose systemic failures in pandemic preparedness that, if left unaddressed, could leave health systems vulnerable when the next global health emergency strikes.

🏷️ UK / Health / Pandemic

### #4 | US Judge Voids Trump's $1.8bn IRS Settlement, Calls It an 'Improper' Deal
A US federal judge has voided a legal settlement between President Donald Trump and federal agencies that granted him immunity from tax audits and established a since-abandoned $1.8 billion "anti-weaponisation" fund. District Judge Kathleen Williams ruled on Monday that Trump's lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) was filed for an "improper purpose" and described the settlement as a bid to "provide some legitimacy to an agreement to confer immunity to people and entities affiliated with the President." The judge also referred Trump attorney Alejandro Brito to the Florida bar for potential disciplinary action and barred a second lawyer from practising in the Southern District of Florida for at least a year. The ruling could allow the IRS to resume audits into Trump's tax claims, which had been frozen under the now-voided settlement.

**Why it matters:** The ruling represents a significant judicial rebuke of presidential self-dealing, with implications for executive power and the independence of tax enforcement in the United States.

🏷️ US Politics / Law / Trump

### #5 | Singapore Court Orders Bloomberg to Pay $356,000 to Ministers in Defamation Case
Singapore's High Court has ordered Bloomberg and one of its reporters to pay S$460,000 (US$356,000) in damages to two government ministers — K Shanmugam and Tan See Leng — over a 2024 article about luxury property transactions. The piece, titled "Singapore Mansion Deals Are Increasingly Shrouded in Secrecy," examined how wealthy buyers obscured their purchases of Good Class Bungalows through shell companies and non-caveated deals. Judge Audrey Lim ruled that when read as a whole, the article implied the ministers "took advantage" of existing regulations to deal their properties in a "non-transparent manner" and to "avoid scrutiny that might extend to the possibility of money laundering." Bloomberg's editor-in-chief said the outlet was "very disappointed" but would respect the ruling. The article has been taken down from Bloomberg's website.

**Why it matters:** The case reignites debate over press freedom in Singapore, where the government has a long history of successfully suing both domestic critics and international media outlets for defamation.
