### #1 | Iran Closes Strait of Hormuz as US Launches Third Round of Strikes in a Week

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) announced the closure of the Strait of Hormuz "until further notice" on Sunday, triggering a massive escalation as the United States launched a third wave of strikes hitting 140 Iranian military targets including missile and drone sites, communication networks, and coastal surveillance installations. The IRGC said it had hit a Cyprus-flagged vessel, the MV GFS Galaxy, firing a naval cruise missile after the ship allegedly ignored instructions to follow an approved route through Iranian waters — the crew was forced to abandon ship and one remains missing. In retaliation, Iran struck the Prince Hassan Air Base in Jordan, claiming to have destroyed its command centre and MQ-9 drone hangars, while missiles and drones also targeted the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, and Bahrain. US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth declared: "Iran made a poor choice. Now they pay." Iran's parliamentary speaker and chief nuclear negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned on X that "the era of one-sided deals is OVER." The violence comes as new Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei issued his first public statement since his father's funeral, calling vengeance "the will of the nation." President Trump said talks would continue and mediators are working to revive the ceasefire process.

**Why it matters:** The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20% of global oil transit. A sustained closure threatens to send energy prices spiralling and marks the most dangerous US-Iran confrontation since the killing of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei in February.

### #2 | US Senator Lindsey Graham Dies at 71 After 'Brief and Sudden Illness'

Republican Senator Lindsey Graham of South Carolina, one of the most influential voices on US foreign policy and a close ally of President Donald Trump, died Saturday evening following what his office described as a "brief and sudden illness." He was 71. Graham had just returned from Kyiv on Friday, where he met Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky to work on a Russia sanctions bill. Zelensky said he was "deeply saddened," noting Graham had visited Ukraine 10 times since Russia's full-scale invasion. Trump paid tribute, calling Graham "a true American Patriot" who would be "greatly missed." Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Graham understood that "the security of Israel and America are inseparable" and that Israel had lost "one of its greatest friends." Graham's death leaves a powerful Senate seat vacant and removes a hawkish voice who consistently pushed for military intervention abroad — from Iraq to Iran — while remaining one of Trump's staunchest defenders after an early rivalry.

**Why it matters:** Graham's death reshapes the Senate's foreign policy balance and removes a key bridge between Trump's White House and the Republican establishment at a moment of intense international crisis.

### #3 | Typhoon Bavi Slams China: 1.7 Million Evacuated, 17 Dead in Philippines

Typhoon Bavi made landfall in China's Zhejiang province on Sunday, forcing the evacuation of more than 1.7 million people — the second typhoon to strike the country in a single week. The storm reached Hangzhou by Sunday morning after battering Japan's remote Ryukyu islands, injuring at least five and cutting power to thousands, and brushing past northern Taiwan with heavy rains that triggered landslide warnings. Schools, businesses, and outdoor activities were suspended across Zhejiang, while 400 flights and dozens of train services were cancelled. The city of Wenzhou, home to 10 million, was near the storm's path. "We could hear roof tiles and tree branches falling," said resident Li Liangxing. Bavi previously killed 17 people in the Philippines through landslides. Forecasters expect the storm to move toward eastern Anhui on Monday, then toward the Yellow Sea by Tuesday. China is still reeling from Typhoon Maysak earlier in the week, which killed 39 people, destroyed livestock, and spawned two rare tornadoes in Hubei province.

**Why it matters:** Back-to-back typhoons are straining China's disaster response systems, devastating agriculture, and highlighting how climate-driven extreme weather is intensifying across East Asia.

### #4 | Spain Wildfires Kill 12, British Couple Rescued with 40% Burns

Devastating wildfires in Spain's Almería province have claimed 12 lives, including four believed to be British nationals, as flames tore through 6,600 hectares. A British couple were found semi-conscious at the bottom of a ravine near the village of Bédar with burns covering 40% of their bodies — Civil Guard officers followed the sound of distant cries for help in the early hours of Friday morning. "Being able to call out in the condition they were in was a titanic effort," said rescuer Rafael Zea. On Sunday, Andalusia's regional president announced the fire had been contained and its perimeter secured, with 600 of nearly 1,500 evacuees allowed to return home. But anger is building over the emergency response: residents say no text-message alert was sent despite the technology being available, and authorities' claims that victims ignored evacuation routes have been disputed. "We've lived here for three years and there has never been information that you should take this road if there is a fire — never," said resident Emma Mitchell.

**Why it matters:** The fires follow Europe's record-breaking June heatwave and underscore the deadly consequences of extreme weather as summer temperatures continue to climb across the Mediterranean.

### #5 | World Cup 2026: Bellingham Double Sends England Past Norway Into Semifinals

Jude Bellingham scored twice as England came from behind to beat Norway 2-1 after extra time in Miami, booking a semifinal clash against Argentina — the first meeting between the two footballing rivals at a World Cup since 1998. Andreas Schjelderup had given Norway a shock lead in the searing 30°C Florida heat, but Bellingham equalised with a moment of individual brilliance just before half-time, slaloming through the defence and finishing on his weaker left foot. Norway had a second goal controversially disallowed after VAR spotted a foul by Erling Haaland on his Manchester City teammate Elliott Anderson. Bellingham pounced on a goalkeeping error to net his sixth goal of the tournament and send England into their first semifinal since 2018. Manager Thomas Tuchel, however, was blunt: "The result is fantastic but I'm not happy with the performance." England vs Argentina takes place Wednesday. In other World Cup news, South African midfielder Jayden Adams, 25, tragically died after featuring in his nation's run to the round of 32. Meanwhile, Conor McGregor suffered a knee injury just one minute into his UFC 329 rematch with Max Holloway in Las Vegas, with the referee stopping the contest after McGregor fell to the mat three times.

**Why it matters:** England vs Argentina in a World Cup semifinal is the kind of fixture that stops the world — and Bellingham, at just 23, is delivering on the grandest stage.
