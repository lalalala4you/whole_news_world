### #1 | US Launches Fresh Strikes on Iran as Trump Warns Tehran It 'Better Behave'

The United States launched a fresh wave of strikes against Iranian military targets on Wednesday evening, marking the second wave of daylight attacks as the conflict in the Strait of Hormuz intensifies. US Central Command said the strikes targeted Iranian coastal defences and cruise missile storage and launch sites on Greater Tunb Island, further degrading Iran's ability to attack commercial shipping in the strategic waterway. The US military also reimposed a blockade on Iranian ports — an arrangement that had been lifted under a fragile memorandum of understanding struck last month — and redirected two commercial vessels attempting to transit toward Iranian coastal areas.

President Donald Trump, speaking at a defence summit, said Iran was "not happy right now" and that Tehran "wants to settle so badly." But he also warned he has yet to decide whether to "finish it off." Iran's top negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf responded defiantly, telling state media that Iran's national security depended on maintaining its arrangements in the strait and that the country had "no reason" to abide by any deal that brought it no benefit. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps further warned the US to "expect the closure of other oil and gas export routes that serve the interests of the United States and its allies."

Global oil prices have surged sharply as tanker traffic has virtually stalled through the Strait of Hormuz, which handles roughly one-fifth of the world's oil supply. The escalation comes just a day after Iran claimed it had struck US military targets in Bahrain and Kuwait, underscoring how rapidly the preliminary ceasefire has collapsed. The UN human rights chief previously warned that deliberately targeting civilian infrastructure would constitute a war crime, after Trump threatened to bomb Iran's bridges and power plants in April.

**Why it matters:** The collapse of the US-Iran ceasefire threatens to ignite a full-scale regional war that could severely disrupt global energy supplies through the world's most critical oil chokepoint.

### #2 | England Suffer World Cup Semi-Final Heartbreak Against Argentina

England's dream of reaching their first men's World Cup final in 60 years ended in devastating fashion on Wednesday night as Argentina came from behind to win 2-1 in Atlanta. The Three Lions took an early lead through Harry Kane, but goals from Enzo Fernández and substitute Lautaro Martínez in the final 15 minutes sent Argentina through to face Spain in Sunday's final — and left an entire nation heartbroken. Manager Thomas Tuchel is facing sharp criticism for what BBC pundits described as "passive" and "negative" defensive tactics after England crumbled under sustained Argentine pressure in the second half.

Lionel Messi, at 39, delivered yet another masterclass — pulling the strings from the right wing and setting up decisive plays in what BBC Sport called "the king's" performance. Argentina are now one win away from claiming back-to-back World Cup titles after their 2022 triumph in Qatar. England defender Dan Burn said the defeat would "haunt" him, while Prince William urged the team to "hold their heads high," adding that the squad had "inspired us all."

The match was marred by post-game controversy after Argentina players celebrated by waving a banner supporting their country's sovereignty claims over the Falkland Islands (Islas Malvinas). FIFA confirmed it has opened disciplinary proceedings against the Argentine Football Association, which could result in a fine ahead of the final. The incident has added a sharp political edge to what was already one of the most charged rivalries in world football.

**Why it matters:** England's semi-final defeat — their third in four tournaments — has reignited a national debate about whether the men's team can ever get over the line, while Argentina-Messi mythology grows ever larger.

### #3 | China Surpasses US in Global Favourability for First Time, Pew Survey Finds

For the first time since the Pew Research Center began tracking global attitudes toward the two superpowers in 2002, more people around the world now hold a favourable view of China than of the United States. The non-partisan think tank's survey of over 42,000 people across 36 countries, conducted between February and May 2026, found that in 25 of those nations, favourable views of China exceeded those of the US. Countries that saw the biggest swings toward China included Spain, Indonesia, Italy, Greece, and Canada. Only six nations still favour the US more — among them staunch allies Poland, the Philippines, South Korea, India, Japan, and Israel.

The findings come amid what analysts describe as mounting global anxiety over the volatility of US foreign policy under President Trump, whose second term has been marked by renewed hostilities with Iran, threats to annex Greenland, and the dramatic capture of Venezuela's Nicolás Maduro. "The volatility of US policy, including the use of force and the resulting economic harm, has put many on edge," said Chong Ja Ian, a non-resident scholar with Carnegie China. He added that Beijing has "been working hard to burnish its image," especially across the developing world.

Confidence in both Trump and Xi Jinping remained generally low, though Xi scored higher in most countries. A median of 75% of respondents in surveyed middle-income countries felt the US interfered a great deal or a fair amount in other nations' affairs, compared with only 45% who said the same of China. Favorable views of China reached record highs in Italy, Spain, Indonesia, Malaysia, Nigeria, Turkey, Mexico, and Colombia. Pakistan was the most pro-China country surveyed, with around 90% holding favourable views.

**Why it matters:** The historic shift in global public opinion signals a potentially generational realignment of soft power, with profound implications for trade alliances, diplomatic influence, and the future of the US-led international order.

### #4 | Russian Strikes Kill 14 Across Ukraine as Kyiv Targets Black Sea Fleet

At least 14 civilians were killed across Ukraine on Wednesday as Russia launched a wave of drone and missile attacks, striking the Black Sea port city of Odesa for a fifth consecutive day. Three people died when a missile hit a multi-storey residential building in Odesa, while additional casualties were reported in Sumy, Zaporizhzhia, and three other south-eastern regions. The United Nations Human Rights Monitoring Mission reported that at least 293 civilians were killed and nearly 2,000 injured in Ukraine in June alone, with long-range weapons accounting for 45% of deaths — most occurring far from the front line in urban centres.

In a dramatic counter-escalation, Ukraine's military said its drones struck 20 Russian vessels overnight in the Black Sea, including 17 oil tankers. The attacks come as both sides engage in an increasingly intense maritime campaign targeting each other's energy and export infrastructure. Russia has been forced to restrict shipping in the Sea of Azov, a route handling roughly a quarter of its grain exports, following sustained Ukrainian strikes on its vessels there.

The battlefield developments were matched by political upheaval in Kyiv, where Prime Minister Yulia Svyrydenko and Defence Minister Mykhailo Fedorov — widely credited with modernising Ukraine's armed forces — both submitted their resignations. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen arrived in Kyiv on Wednesday for talks on EU-Ukraine defence cooperation and Ukraine's accession pathway, as the country navigates one of its most turbulent periods since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022.

**Why it matters:** The simultaneous intensification of Black Sea warfare and a major government reshuffle in Kyiv signals that the conflict is entering a dangerous new phase, with energy infrastructure and maritime trade routes now firmly at the centre of the war.

### #5 | Bangkok Bar Fire Kills 32, Exposing Lethal Safety Loopholes

At least 32 people are now confirmed dead after a devastating fire ripped through the Rong Beer Na Ladprao pub in Bangkok on Sunday night, in what has become Thailand's deadliest entertainment venue blaze since the 2009 Santika Club fire that killed 67. Survivors described a sudden blast of flame across the crowded room that sent hundreds of revellers scrambling toward a single narrow exit. Thick, toxic smoke — likely from flammable soundproofing materials — quickly filled the venue, and many of the dead were found near the rear exits, which investigators say were partially blocked.

The tragedy has exposed a massive regulatory loophole in Thailand's safety laws. Stricter fire regulations — including fire-resistant insulation, outward-opening exits, illuminated signage, and sprinkler systems — were introduced after the 2009 Santika disaster, but they apply only to venues in officially designated entertainment zones. Bangkok has just three such zones. The vast majority of pubs and clubs, including Rong Beer Na Ladprao, operate under restaurant licences and are exempt from those standards. Bangkok Governor Chadchart Sittipunt acknowledged the "lethal" gap and ordered random inspections of all venues functioning as pubs, but with thousands of such establishments citywide, enforcement capacity remains in serious question.

Professor Worsak Kanok-Nukulchai of the Asian Institute of Technology, who issued warnings after an almost identical fire killed 26 people at the Mountain B club in Sattahip four years ago, said he now finds himself repeating the same alarms. "The key question is not just what caused the accident, but why a spark of fire that starts in one place leads to a tragedy which costs so many lives." Corruption among local officials and police — widely understood to accept payments to turn a blind eye to unlicensed club operations — further undermines any prospect of meaningful reform.

**Why it matters:** The third catastrophic nightclub fire in Thailand in 17 years illustrates a deadly cycle of disaster, promises, and inaction — and raises urgent questions about whether the country can ever break it before the next tragedy strikes.
